Editorial responsibility

Who stands behind the numbers, how they are produced, and where a machine is and is not involved.

The short version

Every figure merona publishes is computed by deterministic code from immutable public snapshots — not estimated, not modelled, not generated. Everything written about those figures is reviewed and approved by a named human before publication, and that human is accountable for it.

How the figures are produced

merona's numbers are not opinions and not predictions. They come out of a fixed pipeline:

  • Settlement records are read directly from six public blockchains and written to a dated snapshot.
  • Scoping, wash detection and cycle detection run as deterministic algorithms over that snapshot — graph decomposition and arithmetic thresholds, published in full. The same snapshot always yields the same answer, for us and for you.
  • Each published figure carries the SHA-256 of the snapshot it came from, so it can be re-derived independently, later, without trusting us.
No figure on this site is produced by a language model, a statistical estimate, a projection or a generative system of any kind. If a number cannot be derived from a snapshot, it does not get published. Where we do not know something, we publish UNKNOWN rather than inventing a value — that rule is enforced in the scoring code itself, not just in policy.

Where a machine assists, and where it does not

We use AI coding assistants to help write and review software, and to help draft prose — documentation, methodology write-ups, report narrative and site copy. We think saying so is more useful than pretending otherwise. What matters is the boundary:

  • Never used to produce data. No figure, score, grade, flag or feed entry is generated, estimated or filled in by an AI system. Those come from the deterministic pipeline above, or they do not exist.
  • Never published unreviewed. Drafted prose is read line by line, checked against the underlying data, edited or rejected, and approved by the person named above before it goes out. That review is substantive — claims get cut when the data does not support them, and figures in prose get traced back to the snapshot that produced them.
  • Corrections outrank convenience. When review catches an error after publication, the correction is published in place, dated, with what changed and why.

Corrections

Corrections are published, not buried. When a figure is withdrawn or restated, the original stays visible alongside the correction and the reasoning, because a measurement service that quietly edits its history is worth nothing. We have done this to our own headline numbers — a chain-level clean figure published on 30 July 2026 was withdrawn the next day and restated after a payer-side trace showed our filters had missed a manufactured structure. That episode is documented in full in the public repository.

If you think something here is wrong, write to hello@merona.io with the reasoning. Substantive challenges get recomputed and answered. Corrections we accept get published under our own name, not attributed away.

What we do not do

  • We do not publish figures we cannot re-derive.
  • We do not present flags as accusations. A flag records what the data shows — a cycle, a mismatch, a rotation — not a conclusion about anyone's intent.
  • We do not accept payment to change, suppress or improve a score, and there is no mechanism by which that could happen: scores are a function of the snapshot.
  • We do not use synthetic or generated media to represent real people or events.
