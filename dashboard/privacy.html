The short version
  • merona indexes public blockchain data. It does not collect data about you to build a profile of you.
  • No accounts, no signup, no tracking cookies, no advertising, no analytics trackers, no data sold or shared.
  • The free wallet check is anonymous by design — the thing you look up is stripped from our logs before they are written.
  • Wallet addresses can be personal data. We say so plainly below, and we tell you what to do if one of them is yours.

Who is responsible

merona is operated by a single independent operator. Contact for anything on this page, including data-protection requests: hello@merona.io. Requests are answered by a human, normally within a few days and always within one month.

What merona reads

  • Public blockchain records. Settlement transactions on six chains — sender and recipient addresses, amounts, timestamps, transaction hashes. This data is already public and permanent on those chains; merona reads it, it does not create it and cannot alter or remove it there.
  • Public endpoint catalogs and the endpoints themselves. Directory listings of x402 services, plus a nightly probe of each listed endpoint that records the payout address it advertises and the one it actually asks for.

Both sources are public. merona does not buy data, scrape private systems, or receive data from third parties about individuals.

Wallet addresses and personal data

A wallet address is a pseudonym, not a name. But European regulators have been clear that on-chain identifiers can be personal data where they allow a natural person to be identified indirectly — the EDPB confirmed this in its blockchain guidelines adopted in July 2026. We do not pretend otherwise.

Most addresses merona publishes belong to services, relayers and companies. Some may belong to individuals. Where an address relates to an identifiable person, our processing of it is subject to the GDPR, and the rights set out below apply to it.

merona does not attempt to link addresses to real-world identities. We do not run deanonymisation, clustering-to-identity, or identity-resolution of any kind. Where a name appears alongside an address, it is because the operator published that link themselves — in a public catalog, a registry entry, or a self-declaration to us.

Why we are allowed to publish it

Our lawful basis is legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR): making the payment integrity of a public financial rail verifiable, so that a party about to send funds can check where those funds actually go, and so that manufactured volume is not mistaken for real commerce.

We have weighed that against the interests of the people whose addresses appear. Points that matter in the balance: the underlying data is already public and permanent; we publish recorded facts (what an endpoint advertised, what it asked for, what settled on chain) rather than allegations about people; we do not attach identities; the information concerns commercial conduct on a public rail rather than private life; and anyone affected can object using the process below.

What our servers record about visitors

  • Web server logs. IP address, timestamp, requested path, response code and user agent — the ordinary record any web server keeps, retained for security and abuse investigation and then rotated out.
  • The free check is exempt from that by design. For /check and /probe, the q= parameter is deleted before the log line is written. What you looked up never lands on disk. We cannot tell you which wallets have been checked, because we do not know.
  • Aggregate usage counters. A count of requests per route per day. Route and date only — no IP, no identifier, nothing that could distinguish one visitor from another. Kept for 30 days.
  • API request records. For the paid API, we log the calling identity (API key name, x402 payer address, or IP for unauthenticated calls), the path and the status code, so that rate limits and billing are auditable. Paths are truncated and rotated.
  • No cookies are set. No third-party scripts, fonts, tag managers, pixels or analytics services are loaded on any merona page.

Payments

Paid API calls are settled in USDC over the x402 protocol. Settlement happens on a public blockchain through a third-party facilitator; the transaction and the paying address are public on chain by the nature of that rail. We record the payer address and the transaction reference against the call. We never see or hold card details, bank details or any custodial funds, and there is no account to create.

Retention, and an honest word about permanence

merona's published figures rest on nightly snapshots that are committed immutably, so that any number we publish can be re-derived by anyone, later, without trusting us. That auditability is the product. It also means our historical record is append-only by design.

In practice: we can remove an address from the live feed, the published flags and the scoring surface, and we will. We cannot rewrite past immutable snapshots, and nobody can remove anything from the underlying blockchains — that is a property of those chains, not a choice we made. If you exercise a right below, we will tell you exactly what we removed and what we could not, rather than implying more than we can deliver.

Your rights

If you are in the EU, EEA or UK, you have the right to access the data we hold about you, to have inaccurate data corrected, to have data erased, to restrict or object to processing, and to lodge a complaint with your national data-protection authority.

Because we do not hold identities, we cannot look you up by name. To make a request about an address, write to hello@merona.io and show control of it — a signed message from that address is the cleanest proof, and it is the same mechanism used for relayer self-declaration.

Objections are taken seriously and answered substantively. If you believe a figure or a flag concerning you is wrong, say so with the reasoning: we recompute, and where we were wrong we correct the record publicly rather than quietly. Corrections are published, not buried — that policy predates this page and applies to everyone, including us.

Where the data is

merona runs on a single dedicated server in the European Union (Germany), fronted by a content-delivery network for TLS and abuse protection. Email is handled by a standard mail provider. We do not transfer personal data to third parties for their own purposes, and we do not sell data under any circumstances.

Children

merona is a technical service for developers and operators of automated payment systems. It is not directed at children and we do not knowingly process children's data.

Changes

If this notice changes materially, the version and date at the top change with it and the previous version stays available in our public repository — the same way our data does.
